## The usual stages (as described by third parties)

Source: [Levels.fyi blog by an anonymous former Google recruiter, written Dec 2020](https://www.levels.fyi/blog/google-software-engineer-interview-process.html) [Third-party, old]. Check with your recruiter. Other guides agree on the shape ([IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/tech/google-interview-process)).

1. **Resume review.** A person reads it against the job's "minimum" and "preferred" qualifications.
2. **Recruiter screen.** A call about your background and the process.
3. **Phone or video screen (about 45 minutes).** One coding problem in a shared editor. Interviewer writes feedback, usually within about 2 days.
4. **Onsite loop.** Described as five 45-minute interviews (digital since 2020 in that article). Deeper versions of the phone screen, sometimes two coding problems per interview.
5. **Hiring committee (HC).** Senior engineers who did NOT interview you read all feedback. They vote hire or no hire. It can also suggest a lower level or ask for one more interview.
6. **Team matching.** After HC approval, you talk (30 to 45 minute "fit calls") to managers who have open roles. This can add weeks. The same article says interview scores stay valid about one year.
7. **Offer.** Compensation team sets the package by level and location.

A 2026 Substack says team matching sometimes happens before the HC now. That contradicts the usual order. It is one source, so treat it with care ([dglearning](https://dglearning.substack.com/p/inside-the-google-2026-loop-rounds)).

Timeline in that article: 4 to 6 weeks, sometimes longer.

## What each interview type tests

**Coding / DSA (data structures and algorithms).**
- Simple meaning: solve a problem with code, on a shared editor, while explaining.
- Google problems are often custom and a bit vague. You must ask questions first ([Aced, third-party](https://www.aced.io/blog/google-ai-coding-interview)).
- Prepare: arrays, strings, hash maps, trees, graphs, dynamic programming basics, recursion. Say the plan before the code. Test with a small example.

**System design.**
- The same Levels.fyi article says L3 and L4 had four DSA interviews plus one Googleyness interview, and L5 and L6 had three DSA, one system design and one Googleyness. So for L3/L4, system design may not be a separate round. This is 2020 and anecdotal. Check with your recruiter.

**Googleyness and Leadership (G&L).**
- Simple meaning: a behavioural interview. They want to know if you are a good teammate and handle unclear situations. Practise with the STAR method.

## 2026 changes you should know (all [Third-party], not official)

- **In-person rounds are coming back for some roles.** Reports in 2025 say Google would bring back at least one in-person round for some engineering roles because of AI cheating worries ([sightsinplus](https://www.sightsinplus.com/news/hiring/google-reinstates-in-person-interviews-to-counter-ai-cheating)). That is one news outlet. Ask your recruiter if your round is on video or in person.
- **AI-assisted coding pilot.** Aced reports Google is piloting a "code comprehension" round where candidates read, debug and improve existing code with Google's Gemini assistant, for junior and mid-level candidates on select US teams. What is judged: how you prompt, how you check the AI's answer, and whether you own the final code ([Aced](https://www.aced.io/blog/google-ai-coding-interview)). I do not know if this applies to India or to your role. Ask.
- **Mixed signals.** Other blogs say some Google teams ban AI in interviews. The truth probably depends on role and team.
- **How to prepare for AI-allowed rounds:** (1) Reproduce the bug first. (2) Make a guess about the cause. (3) Ask the AI a narrow question. (4) Read every line it changes. (5) Run tests. (6) Explain out loud why you trust the result.

## Levels: L3, L4 and where your experience fits

- Google engineering levels start at L3 (entry) and go up: L4, L5 (Senior), and so on.
- Crowd-sourced US data on Levels.fyi (updated 15 Nov 2026): L3 typical 1 to 3 years, L4 typical 4 to 5 years, L5 typical 8 to 10 years ([Levels.fyi Google](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/google/salaries/software-engineer)) [Third-party, US data, self-reported].
- Google India job post, Software Engineer III, Google Cloud (June 2026): minimum 2 years of software development experience (or 1 year with an advanced degree); 3 years with data structures and algorithms is "preferred" (from a search result, I did not open the posting; see [Byp network listing](https://www.byp.network/jobs/543303037-software-engineer-iii-google-cloud-at-google)) [Third-party listing].
- Naming: Google often calls L3 "Software Engineer II" and L4 "Software Engineer III". I believe this is right, but I did not verify it in an official source.
- With about 3.5 years, you sit between "typical L3" and "typical L4". The recruiter and the committee decide. The committee can offer a lower level than you applied for ([Levels.fyi blog](https://www.levels.fyi/blog/google-software-engineer-interview-process.html)). Your Senior Software Engineer title (July 2026) and your platform scope help, but Google levels by interview results, not by your current title. [General advice]
