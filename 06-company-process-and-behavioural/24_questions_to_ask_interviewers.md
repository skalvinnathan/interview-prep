# 24. Questions to ask interviewers

## Why this matters

At the end, almost every interviewer says: "Do you have any questions for me?" Amazon's guide says to prepare thoughtful questions for your interviewers ([Official](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-interview-guide)). Good questions show you are serious and help you decide if you want the job.

Rules:
1. Prepare 5 questions per company. Ask 2 or 3 per interviewer. Do not repeat the same question to every person.
2. Ask about **work**, not only about money or holidays.
3. Do not ask something that is on the careers page. That shows you did not read.
4. Write the answers down after the call. They help you compare offers.
5. It is fine to say, "I am still thinking, I will email you."

(All of this is [General advice].)

## To the recruiter
- What are the next steps and how long does each take?
- What level is this role? (For example SDE II or L4.) Is the level fixed or could it change after the interviews?
- How many rounds are there? What does each one test?
- Is any round done with an AI assistant, or is AI not allowed?
- Is the round on video or in person?
- Is this role open in India only, or can it be done abroad? Is there relocation and visa help?
- What is the notice-period expectation and the usual joining time?
- (Salary: ask about the **range of the role**. Do not give your number first. See file 25.)

## To an engineer (peer)
- What does a normal week look like on your team?
- What was the hardest technical problem your team solved this year?
- How do you release code? How often? What happens when a release goes wrong?
- How do you handle on-call? How often are you paged?
- How do you decide what to build and what to postpone?
- What do you wish was better about the codebase or the tools?
- How do new engineers get started in the first month?

## To the hiring manager
- What would success look like in the first 6 months for this role?
- What are the team's biggest challenges now?
- How do you give feedback and how do people get promoted here?
- What kind of engineer does well on your team?
- How is the team split between new features and keeping things running?
- How does the team work with other teams?
- What is something you would change about how the team works?

## For a senior person or a bar-raiser type interviewer
- What do you think makes a person succeed at this company long term?
- How has the company's engineering changed in the last 2 years?
- What makes you stay here?

## For platform, SRE and infrastructure roles (fits your background)
- What does the platform look like: cloud, Kubernetes, CI/CD?
- How do you handle secrets and access control?
- What are your uptime goals? How do you measure them?
- What does the monitoring stack look like?
- What does a blameless post-incident review look like here?
- How much of the work is toil, and what are you doing about it?

## For payments companies (Stripe, Adyen, Klarna, PayPal, Razorpay, PhonePe)
- How do you make sure a payment is never charged twice?
- How do you handle a downstream bank or partner being slow or down?
- How are changes reviewed and approved for money-moving code?

## For AI startups (Smallest AI and similar)
- What is the main thing the team must ship in the next 3 months?
- How big is the engineering team? Who would I work with?
- How do you decide between speed and quality?
- How do you measure the quality of your models in production?
- What does an average week of hiring and onboarding look like?

## For Amazon specifically
- How do teams here use the Leadership Principles day to day?
- What is a recent example of a project that was stopped or changed, and why?
- How does the team use AI tools in its work? (Candidates report AI questions appear in Amazon loops; one source says so, with low confidence.)

## For European companies
- Is the company a recognised visa sponsor? How long does permit processing usually take?
- Is relocation help provided (flights, housing for first weeks)?
- What language is used at work and in the team?
- Is there an expectation to work from the office, and how many days?

## Questions NOT to ask early
- "How soon can I get promoted?"
- "How many holidays do I get?" (Ask the recruiter later.)
- "What does the company do?" (Read before.)
- Anything about firing or layoffs in a negative tone.

## After the interview
Send a short thank-you email within a day. Three lines: thank you, one thing you liked from the talk, and that you look forward to the next step. Amazon calls thank-you notes optional ([Official](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-interview-guide)). If you do not hear back within the stated time, send a polite follow-up to the recruiter (also in Amazon's guide).
