26. Interview day checklists

Three checklists: online (video), in-person, and take-home or online assessment. All are [General advice] unless a source is shown.

Your first rule: read the invitation email twice. The company's email is the truth for your interview (timing, tools, AI rules). Amazon says the same kind of thing for its assessments: the format varies by role, so check your invitation (PracHub summary of Amazon guidance [Third-party]).

A. Online video interview

The day before

  • Confirm the time and the time zone (IST vs the company's zone). Put it in your calendar with two reminders.
  • Open the meeting link. Test it on your laptop.
  • Check the camera, microphone and headphones. Record 20 seconds and play it back.
  • Check internet. Keep your phone hotspot ready as a backup.
  • Charge the laptop and keep the charger plugged in.
  • Update the browser. Close updates that might restart your PC.
  • Prepare the coding editor the company uses (for example a shared editor link). Have a pen and paper.
  • Re-read the job text and your resume. Know every line.
  • Re-read your top 6 stories (your own notes).
  • Write 5 questions to ask (file 24).
  • Ask the recruiter: "Is an AI assistant allowed? Will I code in my own editor?"
  • Sleep 7 hours.

One hour before

  • Water, light snack, toilet.
  • Quiet room, door closed, phone on silent (except the backup hotspot).
  • Light in front of your face, not behind.
  • Clean the desktop. Close chat apps and notifications.
  • Put the camera at eye level.
  • Warm up with one easy coding problem.

During

  • Join 3 to 5 minutes early.
  • Say hello, smile, speak slowly. Short sentences.
  • For coding: (1) repeat the problem, (2) ask about input sizes and edge cases, (3) give an example, (4) say your plan, (5) code, (6) test with a small example, (7) say the time and space cost.
  • Think out loud. Silence is hard for the interviewer.
  • If you are stuck: say what you tried and ask for a hint. A hint is not a failure.
  • If you do not understand a word: "Sorry, could you say that again in a different way?" It is fine. English is your second language and that is normal.
  • Do not read from notes. Do not look at a second screen.
  • Do not use AI unless the rules allow it. If AI is allowed, keep control: small questions, read every line, test.
  • Keep a short note of each question for later.

After

  • Write down all questions and your weak points within one hour.
  • Send a short thank-you within a day (three lines).
  • Follow up politely if you do not hear within the stated time. Amazon, for example, says to follow up with the recruiter if the stated time passes (Official).

B. In-person interview

(Some companies are bringing back at least one in-person round, reported for Google in 2025 by a news outlet, Third-party. Ask your recruiter.)

The day before

  • Confirm address, floor, who to ask for, and a phone number for the security desk.
  • Check the route and travel time in the same traffic hour. Plan to arrive 20 to 30 minutes early.
  • Carry original ID. Many offices need it at security. Ask if they need a printed copy of your resume.
  • Carry 2 printed resumes, a pen, a notebook, water, charger, and your phone with the recruiter's number.
  • Clothes: clean and comfortable. Business casual is common for tech. Choose the usual style of that company if you know it. Avoid new shoes.
  • Prepare a 60-second introduction.

On the day

  • Eat something. Go to the toilet before.
  • Be polite to every person you meet (security, reception). They may give feedback.
  • Greet with a smile and a handshake or a nod.
  • Whiteboard: write big. Say your plan first. Do not rub out your idea in a hurry.
  • Keep notes small. Make eye contact. Speak slowly.
  • Between rounds, drink water and quickly note what happened.
  • Lunch with the team (if any) is usually part of the culture check. Be normal and friendly.
  • Ask your prepared questions.
  • At the end, ask about next steps and timeline.

After

  • Same as online: write down questions, thank-you note, polite follow-up.

C. Take-home task or online assessment (OA)

Before you start

  • Read the instructions fully. Write down: time limit, allowed tools, AI policy, language, how to submit.
  • Do NOT start the timer until you are ready (if you control it). Choose a time when you will not be interrupted.
  • Quiet room, charger, internet checked, notifications off.
  • Close all other tabs. The browser may be logged. Amazon's university OA page, for example, says browser usage is logged and warns against copy-paste (Official).
  • Choose one language you know best.
  • Do the tutorial or practice question if the site gives one.

For a timed online coding test (for example HackerRank)

  • Spend the first 2 minutes reading all questions. Split your time.
  • Start with the question you can do fastest.
  • Write correct code first, then improve speed.
  • Run your code on the sample cases and on 2 edge cases (empty, one item, very large, duplicates).
  • Do not spend more than 60% of your time on one question without progress. Move on, then come back.
  • Leave 10 minutes at the end to run everything again and submit.
  • Check that you submitted every section. (Amazon's SDE II page says all sections must be completed to submit: Official.)

For a repository or AI-assisted task

  • Run the tests first. Note which fail. Reproduce before you ask AI anything.
  • Find the code path from the entry point.
  • Ask the AI narrow questions. Read every change. Do not edit tests to fit your code.
  • Re-run the original failing test, then all tests, then one extra edge case.
  • Keep a short note of what you changed and why. (See file 11 for the longer method.)

For work-style surveys and work simulations

  • Be honest and consistent. Do not try to guess the "right" answer. The same idea appears many times in different words.
  • Read the Leadership Principles before.
  • Do not rush. Read each option fully.

For a take-home project (no timer, or a long timer)

  • Time-box it. Decide: "I will spend at most 4 hours." Tell the company if the task seems longer than they said.
  • Build the smallest working version first. Then improve.
  • Add a few tests.
  • Write a README: how to run, what you built, what you left out, what you would do next.
  • Commit in small steps with clear messages.
  • Do not copy code you cannot explain. Be ready to walk through every part.
  • Submit before the deadline. Confirm that it arrived.

After

  • Note what was hard.
  • Do not discuss exact test questions in public if the company says they are confidential. Respect the rules.
