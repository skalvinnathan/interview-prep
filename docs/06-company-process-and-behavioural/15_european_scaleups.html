15. European scale-ups and visa basics

Companies: Adyen, Booking.com, Spotify, Klarna, Picnic. Checked 15 Nov 2026. I found no official interview descriptions for these. All process facts are [Anecdotal] or [Third-party]. Visa facts come from government pages where I could open them (marked [Official]).

A scale-up is a company that is past the tiny start-up stage and is growing fast.

Part 1: How they interview

Adyen (Amsterdam) - payments company

Sources: Taro, Adyen reports, Glassdoor, Sept 2025, Glassdoor. All [Anecdotal].

  • Recruiter screen (motivation: "Why Adyen?").
  • A coding assessment on HackerRank (one report says 3 hours).
  • A technical interview plus system design (one report: 1.5 hours).
  • For an SE II report: a third round that combined technical and system design, then a leadership interview in Amsterdam.
  • Behavioural questions: why Adyen, past experiences, conflicts and how you handled them.
  • Time: about 3 to 4 weeks in recent reports. Many reviews call it hard. An old 2018 report had a 10% pass rate from 20 reports (old, not reliable).

Booking.com (Amsterdam)

Sources: TechPrep, Glassdoor Feb 2026 Amsterdam, Glassdoor 2021 list of steps. [Anecdotal / Third-party].

  • Steps reported: online coding test (HackerRank), a recruiter call, a technical phone interview, then "virtual business interviews" (a set of interviews, technical and behavioural). One guide says 3 stages (screening, technical assessment, deep-dive rounds), another says 4 to 5.
  • Time: 3 weeks to 2 months in different reports.

Spotify (Stockholm, London and others)

  • I could find only that a recruiter screen is the first step. I found no reliable description of the technical rounds. Individual reviews are mixed and sparse. I cannot say more with a source. Ask your recruiter for the steps, and read the job post.

Klarna (Stockholm) - payments company

Sources: TechPrep, Interview Query, Glassdoor. [Third-party / Anecdotal].

  • Guides list: recruiter screen, a technical screen or coding test, technical interview, system design, behavioural or values interview, hiring manager, offer. 5 to 7 stages.
  • One Glassdoor report lists: HR round, aptitude test, online coding test, pair programming, system design, behaviour round, interview with the future team, offer.
  • Time: about 36 days on average per one prep site; one 2023 Stockholm candidate said 8 weeks. Some say the process felt too long.

Picnic (Amsterdam) - online grocery company

Sources: Glassdoor reports, Dataford (mostly paywalled, could not verify). [Anecdotal].

  • HR screen, a home assignment (take-home coding task), a technical interview. Possibly pair programming and a talk with team leads or the CTO (unverified).
  • Behavioural: "Why Picnic?" Some senior candidates were asked how to make the code process faster.
  • Time: 1 week to 7 weeks in different reports. Reviews say it can feel inconsistent.

What they have in common (from the reports above)

  1. A coding test or take-home early.
  2. System design for experienced candidates.
  3. Motivation and values questions ("Why us?"). Prepare a real answer for each company, using what they build (payments, travel, music, groceries).
  4. Written clear code matters. Tests matter.
  5. Payments companies (Adyen, Klarna) care about reliability, correctness and money. Idempotency, retries, ledgers (see file 12).

How to prepare (General advice)

  • Take-home: read the task, keep the solution small, add tests, write a README. Time-box it (do not spend 20 hours).
  • "Why this company?": 3 sentences. Say what you like in their product, one engineering thing you learned about them, and why your platform skills help.
  • English: speak slowly and in short sentences. It is fine.

Part 2: Visa and relocation basics

Plain words. A work permit is permission to live and work in a country. For these countries, the employer usually applies, or must be approved to hire from abroad. There is a minimum salary that changes every year. If the offered salary is below the minimum, the permit is refused. Always check the date of any number below. Re-check it on the government page before you decide.

Netherlands - Highly skilled migrant (kennismigrant)

  • Needs an employment contract with an IND-recognised sponsor (the employer must be on the IND's register). Only the employer applies. IND must normally decide within 90 days. Fee EUR 423, paid by the employer. [Official: https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/highly-skilled-migrant]
  • 2026 minimum gross monthly salary: EUR 5,942 if age 30 or older, EUR 4,357 if under 30, EUR 3,122 for the reduced criterion (recent graduates and similar). EU Blue Card: EUR 5,942 (reduced criterion EUR 4,754). Holiday allowance is not counted. Amounts change each 1 January. [Official: https://ind.nl/en/required-amounts-income-requirements]
  • 30% ruling (a tax benefit for some foreign hires): in 2026 the tax-free part stays 30%; from 1 January 2027 it is due to become 27%. Sources differ on whether the change is final, and on details. [Third-party: Grant Thornton NL, Forvis Mazars] The IND page I opened does not mention it. Check with the Belastingdienst (Dutch tax office).

Sweden - Work permit

  • From 1 June 2026, your salary must be at least 90% of Sweden's median salary at the time of application. The earlier rule was 80%. Some groups need only 75%: certain occupations on a government list (including certain IT technicians), and employees of qualifying start-ups (under 5 years old, fewer than 100 staff). Extensions: the old 80% rule still applies if you applied between 1 June and 1 December 2026 for an extension of a permit granted before 1 June; from 2 December 2026 the 90% rule applies to extensions too. [Official: https://www.migrationsverket.se/en/newsarchive/newrulesforworkpermitsnowapply.5.7e7ff7f619e5ace55a71e2.html]
  • The amount in kronor: a search summary quoted the Migrationsverket figure as SEK 34,470 per month (90% of a median of about SEK 38,300 published June 2026). I did not open that page, and some sites still show SEK 33,390 (older). Check the figure on Migrationsverket (maintenance requirement page).
  • Pay must also match collective agreements or common practice in your field.

Denmark - Pay Limit Scheme

  • Minimum yearly pay: DKK 552,000 at the 2026 level (adjusted each 1 January). Fixed cash salary, pension payments and paid holiday allowance count. At least 30 hours per week. Salary must go to a Danish bank account in your name. Normal processing 1 month (up to 3). Fee DKK 6,810. Family (spouse or partner, children under 18) can be included. Permit usually 4 years if the job is 4 years or longer. [Official: https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-want-to-apply/Work/Pay-Limit-Scheme]

Ireland - Critical Skills Employment Permit

  • Minimum pay: EUR 40,904 for occupations on the Critical Skills list (a relevant degree needed); EUR 36,848 if the applicant got their qualification in the last 12 months; EUR 68,911 for other occupations with a higher pay floor. ICT professionals are listed as examples of eligible occupations. The job offer must be for at least 2 years. You are expected to stay with the first employer at least 9 months. The holder can bring family; a partner can seek a Dependant/Partner/Spouse permit. After 60 months of residence permission you can apply for long-term residence. [Official: https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/critical-skills-employment-permit/]
  • Check that your exact job title is on the list. The page says to check with the Department and irishimmigration.ie before relying on it.

Germany - EU Blue Card

  • I could not open the official German pages (Make it in Germany and BAMF both blocked my tool). So these numbers are [Third-party], from law-firm and recruiter blogs, and must be checked: for 2026 the general Blue Card salary threshold is about EUR 50,700 per year, and a lower threshold of about EUR 45,934.20 applies to shortage occupations such as IT. Some sources say IT specialists can qualify even without a university degree under a special rule (Section 18g of the Residence Act), with approval by the Federal Employment Agency. Sources: Alda Legal, Jobbatical. Official pages to check: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card
  • I could not verify degree rules or time to permanent residence from an official source.

What to ask the recruiter early

  1. "Does this role come with visa sponsorship and relocation help?"
  2. "Is the employer a recognised sponsor / does it have experience hiring from India?"
  3. "What is the salary band, and is it above the legal minimum for the permit?"
  4. "How long does the permit take, and will it cover my family?"
  5. "Can I start remotely from India while the permit is processed?" (Policies differ.)

Honest limits

  • None of this is legal advice. Visa rules change; numbers change on 1 January and mid-year.
  • Taxes, cost of living and the salary comparison are not covered here. Do not compare gross salary numbers between countries without checking tax and rent.
