25. Salary, notice period and offer negotiation (India and abroad)

Important rules for this file

  • You do NOT give a salary number first. Let the company name the range or the offer first. Reasons are below.
  • I give no India salary ranges. I could not find a reliable, current, official source for SDE II pay in Bengaluru, so I will not invent one. Section 6 shows how to research real numbers yourself.
  • The only numbers here are government minimum salaries for work permits (section 8). They are minimums for visas, not market pay.
  • This is general advice, not legal or tax advice.

1. Words you must know

  • CTC (Cost to Company): the total yearly amount the company spends on you on paper. It is not the money in your bank. It can include fixed pay, variable pay, bonus, provident fund (PF), insurance, gratuity and sometimes stock.
  • Fixed pay: guaranteed monthly part.
  • Variable pay / bonus: paid only if conditions are met (company or personal performance). Ask how often it is actually paid in full.
  • In-hand (take-home): what reaches your bank after tax and PF each month.
  • Joining bonus: a one-time bonus when you join. It is often paid back if you leave within a set time (for example 1 year). Check the clause.
  • RSU: Restricted Stock Units. Company shares given to you over time (for example over 4 years). They are worth the share price on the day they become yours (vest). Ask the vesting schedule.
  • ESOP: stock options at a start-up. Often worth nothing if the company does not succeed. Ask the strike price and the last company valuation.
  • Gratuity: a retirement-type payment from the employer after 5 years in India.
  • Level / band: the rank (for example SDE II, L4). Level matters more than small salary differences because pay, bonus and stock bands depend on it.
  • Total compensation (TC): fixed + variable + stock + bonuses, averaged per year. Compare offers using TC and also in-hand.

2. Why you do not give the number first

  • If you name a number lower than their budget, you lose money. If you name a higher number, they may stop the talk.
  • The company knows the range for the role. You do not.
  • In India, recruiters often ask for "current CTC" and "expected CTC". You are allowed to be polite and firm.

What to say (simple scripts):

  1. Recruiter: "What is your expected salary?" You: "I would like to understand the range for this role and level first. I am sure we can find something fair. Could you share the range?"

  2. If they insist: "I am looking for a package in line with the market for a [level] role. I would prefer to hear your budget, and then I can tell you if it works."

  3. If they ask for current CTC: "I would rather not focus on my current pay, because this role is a different scope. I am happy to share how my work has grown, and I was promoted to Senior Software Engineer in July 2026." Then move to the role.

  4. If they still require it: you may say that it is a common ask but that you prefer to discuss the range first. If you do share your current pay, share the truth. Do not lie. Companies can check pay details during background verification. [General advice. I could not verify how each company checks; read the offer letter conditions.]

Do not say: "I will take whatever you offer" or "I don't know my worth".

3. When an offer arrives

  1. Say thank you and show you are happy. "Thank you. I am excited about this."
  2. Ask for the offer in writing with the full breakup: fixed, variable, bonus, joining bonus, RSU or ESOP details, level and title, location, joining date, probation, notice period at the new company.
  3. Do not answer yes or no on the call. Say: "Could I have until [date] to review it carefully?" A few days is normal.
  4. Read the clauses that cost you money later: joining bonus payback, notice period at the new company, non-compete or non-solicit, probation rules, relocation payback, variable pay conditions.
  5. Work out the real take-home (ask HR for a salary breakup sheet, or use an income-tax calculator).
  6. Compare total compensation, level and learning. Not only the top number.

4. How to negotiate (simple steps)

  1. Know your goal and your walk-away line before you talk. Write them down.
  2. Research the market (section 6). Bring facts, not feelings.
  3. Ask once, politely, with reasons. Example: "I am very happy with the offer. Based on the level and the market data I have seen, I was hoping the package could be higher, especially [fixed pay / joining bonus / stock]. Is there flexibility?"
  4. Negotiate more than salary. If fixed pay is stuck, you can ask for: joining bonus, a higher level, more stock, a later joining date, notice-period buyout help, relocation help, a review after 6 months (get it in writing).
  5. A competing offer is the strongest tool if it is real. Never invent one.
  6. Be calm and kind. The person you negotiate with is your future colleague's recruiter.
  7. When you agree, get the final numbers in writing before you resign.

5. Notice period (India)

Sources are [Third-party]: Instahyre, Business Today explainer, The People's Board, Zoho Payroll. They agree on the main points. Some are marketing pages, so check your own contract and ask a lawyer if there is a dispute.

  • Notice period is usually 30 to 90 days. It is decided by your employment contract, not by one national rule.
  • Employers usually cannot force you to keep working, but they can claim money (damages), deduct unpaid notice from your final payment if the contract allows it, or withhold your relieving letter.
  • Relieving letter matters because many employers need it for background verification.
  • Buyout: you pay your old company the pay for the notice days you will not serve. Often the new company offers to pay it. Check your offer letter and your contract.
  • Waiver: you may ask your manager and HR to shorten the notice. Get it in writing (an HR email), not just a chat.
  • Do not abscond (leave without telling). It can break background verification.
  • Do not resign until you have the signed offer letter and the written final numbers.
  • When you negotiate your joining date, tell the new company your real notice period. Many will wait 30 to 60 days. Ask early about buyout support.

6. How to research real salary numbers (do this yourself)

I could not give you verified ranges. These are places to look. All are crowd-sourced (people type in their own pay), so they can be wrong or old. Use at least three sources and look for the most recent data.

  • Levels.fyi: https://www.levels.fyi (select India, Bengaluru, the company and level). On my test it listed Google US medians by level and last-updated 15 Nov 2026, but not Bengaluru, so you must filter by location yourself.
  • Glassdoor salaries and interview reviews.
  • AmbitionBox (India) and Blind (private forum).
  • The recruiter's own range (best source). Ask: "What is the budget range for this role?"
  • Check the job post. Some posts list a pay range (I did not verify which countries or companies do).

How to use the data: write the 25th, 50th and 75th percentile for your level and city. Your target is "median to 75th", depending on your interviews and competing offers. Ask for the range for total compensation and also the fixed part.

7. Offers abroad: extra things to check

  • Gross vs net: gross is before tax. Taxes and social costs differ by country. Do not compare gross salaries between countries. Compare net money after tax and rent.
  • Cost of living: rent in Amsterdam, Stockholm, Copenhagen, Dublin and Munich can be much higher than in Bengaluru.
  • Relocation package: flights, temporary housing, shipping, visa fees, tax advice. Ask what is included, and whether you must pay it back if you leave early.
  • Visa: is the employer a sponsor? Who pays the fees? The permit usually depends on the employer. If you change job, you may need a new permit or approval. For the Netherlands, only an IND-recognised sponsor can apply and the employer applies [Official, IND]. For Ireland the permit expects you to stay with the first employer for at least 9 months [Official, Irish enterprise department].
  • Family: can your partner or children come? Can your partner work? (Ireland and Denmark pages say family can come; details differ. See file 15.)
  • Start date: how long will the visa take? Agree what happens if it is delayed.
  • Probation and notice abroad: rules differ by country and contract (I did not verify them country by country). Ask the recruiter and read the contract.
  • Tax benefit in the Netherlands: the 30% ruling is a tax benefit for some foreign hires. It is 30% in 2026 and is due to become 27% from 2027; sources differ on details (Grant Thornton NL, Forvis Mazars). Check with a tax advisor.

9. A short checklist

  • I did not give a number first.
  • I have the offer in writing with full breakup.
  • I know my notice period and buyout rules.
  • I checked the joining-bonus and relocation payback clauses.
  • I compared total compensation, level, learning and team.
  • I asked once, politely, with reasons.
  • I have the final numbers in writing before resigning.
  • For abroad: visa sponsor, permit minimum, family, taxes and rent checked.
